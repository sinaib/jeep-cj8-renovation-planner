- **Valves (cold):** IN 0.10–0.15mm · EX 0.15–0.20mm ✅ confirmed in manual

## Origin story & plan philosophy (from the co-work transcript, 2026-07)

- Sinai found the bike stored in an **indoor/underground parking garage** (brick-paved, next to a dusty cream classic car) — complete, very dusty, on stand. Indoor storage = no rain/UV exposure, a meaningful plus vs the Jeep's 8 years outdoors.
- Identification history: from photos alone it was first guessed as a Honda NT650 Hawk/Bros; Sinai said **"רשום MZ"** (it says/is registered MZ) → identified as MZ Skorpion, then **confirmed to the year by the ID-plate photos** (VIN 10th char R = 1994, "MuZ" branding = pre-2001, 35kW plate = standard engine, not the Replica).
- Dash: speedo to 220 km/h + tach + temp gauge (odometer visible in a photo but reading not yet recorded).
- **The plan's philosophy (explicit):** *recommissioning* sits deliberately between "just get it started" (unsafe — brakes/tyres/compression matter too) and "full restoration" (no cosmetics, no chrome/paint, no performance work). Not routine maintenance either — that's a separate later layer. **Ordering principle: risk-first** — long storage kills engines via varnished fuel, dry bore, dead battery, hardened rubber/seals, seized brakes, aged fluids; tasks are sequenced so no step can damage a later stage (clean fuel + fresh oil + charged battery + verified compression → *before* first crank).
- Headline warning throughout: **don't try to start it yet** — the fastest way to ruin a long-stored engine.

## Model lore — known Skorpion weak points (not yet observed on this bike)

- Hard on chains & sprockets; **rear sprocket bolts loosen** (known weak point — check!)
- Electrical gremlins: tail/dash bulbs, erratic rev counter; earths/connectors = most gremlins
- Throttle cable wear (throttle must snap shut)
- Some oil consumption is normal for the 660 single
- MZ-specific cosmetic/chassis parts hard to source; engine parts easy (Yamaha commonality)
